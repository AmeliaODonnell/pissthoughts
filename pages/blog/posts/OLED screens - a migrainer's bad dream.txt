OLED screens: a migrainer's bad dream


it's a beautiful day today. it's sort of a parody of what people think a nice day should be. the sky is vibrant and cyan with fluffy clouds, the trees are (still) a gorgeous mix of green and red and yellow. no cutting wind, just a gentle breeze. 

i've been trying to purge my life of all distraction. i think they purposely lock QOL features behind complex graphical interfaces just so we burn our batteries faster, watch more ads, buy faster hardware. 

i feel as if my idea of minimalism Doesn't really Compute with the rest of the world. in my mind i don't want things sitting there "just in case", i want to know that everything that's there is something i put there. something i use. 

this idea Doesn't Compute with my constant acquisition of things. Nouns - capitalised in German. but i look back on them and i feel i haven't made any nonessential ones. each is an upgrade or replacement, or allows me to do new things. each does one thing and does it well.

most recently: a JVC GR-DVL100 miniDV camcorder. sold cheap as a complete set due to being broken, turned out not to be broken at all and just needed a cleaning tape run through it a few times. Thing is over 20 years old and works fine. 

with tape there's a forced review. dub your favourites and record over the rest. a manageable quantity. you could not say the same for the 5 figure sum stored on my icloud. storing a photo on icloud compounds a disorganisation that's been brewing for 10 years.

George RR Martin writes on a computer running MS DOS. it does all he needs it to do. aside from GUI improvements (which i loathe) and faster hardware (which i can't afford), i can't really think of a way the user experience of a PC has improved since i started using them as a toddler. less viruses. 

maybe it's bluelight glasses, but the internet used to be a thing you had to go sit down at.
